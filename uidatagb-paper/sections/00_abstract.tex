\begin{abstract}
Deep-learning studies on gallbladder ultrasound usually give accuracy and a handful of heatmaps as proof of interpretability, and rarely test whether either survives closer scrutiny. This paper does that scrutiny directly on a ResNet-50 trained for nine-class gallbladder ultrasound classification on the public UIdataGB dataset, and the scrutiny itself changes the headline number. An initial split, de-duplicated only by single-orientation image hashing, gave 93.05\% accuracy; a closer audit found near-identical frames from the same imaging session, and rotation-augmented duplicates the dataset's own release pipeline introduces, still crossing the train/validation boundary. Correcting both and holding out a genuine test set drops mean accuracy across four independently seeded models to 72.56\%~$\pm$~2.35\%, which we report as the real result rather than the inflated one. Five attribution methods (Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, Saliency Maps, Eigen-CAM, Score-CAM) are compared for consistency, faithfulness, stability, a parameter-randomization sanity test, a shortcut audit, and calibration. Grad-CAM++ and Score-CAM are the most reproducible across seeds; on faithfulness all three CAM methods perform similarly, and no single method wins on every metric. A null-control test---cosine similarity between untrained models and between synthetic random heatmaps, which the consistency metric had never been checked against---confirms the corrected consistency score clears both floors by roughly 2.5$\times$ rather than sitting close to them. A separate, targeted audit finds a real batch-correlated shortcut: a classifier trained only to identify which acquisition batch a Carcinoma image came from reaches 82.88\% accuracy (8.3$\times$ chance) even after correcting for cluster-level leakage, and a linear probe on the disease classifier's own features recovers batch identity at 74.75\% (4.5$\times$ chance), directly implicating the classifier these explanations describe. A noise-robustness sweep adds another caution: accuracy on the corrected model falls from 90\% to near chance under still-subtle Gaussian noise. The explanations are measurably faithful to the classifier's behaviour; that is not the same as proof its decisions are clinically reliable, and the shortcut and noise findings are concrete reasons for caution beyond the usual internal-validation caveats. This remains an internal-validation study: it does not establish multicentre generalizability, patient-level validation, or clinician-confirmed localization. Several methodological gaps raised during external review are addressed directly---bootstrap intervals now accompany every XAI table, and direct EXIF timestamp evidence supports the acquisition-session inference used throughout---while others, including a full five-method re-run on the corrected split, remain open and are reported honestly rather than elided.
\end{abstract}
